How the Narratives page is made

Every morning each company's news is turned into vector representations in a latent space, where similarity of meaning is distance. The articles are clustered into narratives, and each narrative's trajectory is followed over thirteen weeks: its birth, its evolution and its death, with the distinct events inside it.

The judgement calls

Duplicate
Two articles whose representations are almost identical count as one.
About the company
Judged per cluster, never per article: a single passing mention is noise, a cluster of them is signal.
Number of narratives
The finest clustering that comes out the same when the articles are resampled and clustered again.
Continuity
A new cluster whose centroid is close to one from last week continues that narrative. If the centroid has drifted far, the narrative has evolved: it is renamed and the old name is kept as “formerly”.
Birth
At least ten recent articles over two or more days, far from every existing centroid and far more common now than in the weeks before.
Alive in a week
At least three articles and at least a fifth of the narrative's peak share. Share, not count, so a quiet news week does not kill every narrative at once.
Death
A weekly re-clustering finds nothing similar to it. The narrative is shown as ended, with its trajectory intact.
Event
Eight or more articles close in latent space and published within days of each other, reporting something that happened rather than an opinion or a prediction.
